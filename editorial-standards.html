Editorial standards

A practical guide to how FED-USA News handles tips, verification, uncertainty, attribution, privacy, and corrections.

News by FED-USA

THE WORKFLOW

From tip to published story

  1. Define the claim. Identify what is being asserted, who is affected, and what evidence could confirm or disprove it.
  2. Check original material. Prefer primary records, direct statements, documents, or first-hand accounts when available. Check dates, location, and context.
  3. Corroborate important facts. Seek independent confirmation for material claims; a repost, anonymous screenshot, or repeated rumor is not independent verification.
  4. Seek a response. Give people or organizations facing substantive criticism a fair opportunity to respond before publication where practical.
  5. Write with calibrated certainty. Attribute claims, label unresolved points, distinguish allegations from established facts, and leave out information that cannot be responsibly checked.
  6. Update transparently. Correct material errors and make the correction visible. Add context when facts change, rather than silently rewriting a consequential point.

Sources and attribution

Readers should be able to tell whether a detail comes from a public record, a named source, a news organization, or an official statement. Anonymous sources are not a shortcut; their identity and basis of knowledge should be assessed, and anonymity explained when it is material.

Privacy and harm

We avoid publishing private addresses, phone numbers, private-person identifiers, or operational details that could increase risk without a clear public-interest reason. We do not turn scanner traffic, social-media claims, or rumors into confirmed news.

AI and automation

Tools can help organize work, but they do not replace checking sources. Every factual assertion in a published story still needs a source and human review. Generated text or images should not be presented as documentary evidence.

Independence

Supporters, community members, and outside organizations may suggest coverage. They do not get to purchase a conclusion, a correction outcome, or editorial control.

Questions or concerns

Use the corrections page for a factual error or contact the newsroom about standards.
