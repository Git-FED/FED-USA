MIDDLE EAST · IRAN BLOCKADE

Iran vows to use all its tools to break U.S. naval blockade

Iran will use all its tools to confront the U.S. naval blockade, Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said at his weekly news conference on Monday, calling the blockade an act of military aggression. The blockade on Iran-related oil exports was imposed April 13, lifted in June after Washington reached a draft understanding with Tehran, then restored July 14 after the agreement collapsed.

Iran claims several oil tankers struck mines in the Strait of Hormuz in recent days, and its Revolutionary Guards said they struck U.S. Patriot radar sites at bases in Bahrain over the weekend. Visible Iranian crude exports have fallen to near zero, and Tehran says it will only reopen the strait when Washington halts hostilities, lifts the blockade and waives sanctions.

MIDDLE EAST · DIPLOMACY

Qatar PM meets Vance and Rubio in Washington to revive U.S.–Iran talks

Qatari Prime Minister Sheikh Mohammed bin Abdulrahman Al Thani met separately Monday with Vice President JD Vance and Secretary of State Marco Rubio at the White House, as Doha seeks to revive U.S.–Iran diplomacy and ease tensions over the naval blockade, a source told AP News.

The meetings come as talks through Qatar remain stalled and Iran says it will not negotiate until its conditions are met, while both sides keep up strikes on shipping and military sites around the Gulf.

UKRAINE · RUSSIA

Russian strike on Zaporizhzhia kills 2, wounds 40

An overnight Russian attack on the Ukrainian city of Zaporizhzhia killed two people and wounded 40 others, including four children, regional governor Mykhailo Semikin said Tuesday. A private home was destroyed and a five-story residential building was partially destroyed, and rescuers were still conducting search and rescue operations as the casualty count rose through the morning.

The strike comes as Ukraine pushes for four-way peace talks with Europe, the United States and Russia before Christmas, and as NATO warns Russia over airspace incursions after missiles crashed in Poland last week.

EUROPE · NATO

NATO deploys surveillance aircraft to Poland after Russian missiles crash

NATO confirmed Monday it is deploying an AWACS early-warning surveillance aircraft to monitor Poland’s eastern region, after Russian missiles crashed in Poland on Friday, raising fears of further airspace incursions.

The deployment follows the missile crash and comes as allies watch for further Russian incursions along NATO’s eastern flank, with surveillance stepped up while the war in Ukraine grinds on.

ASIA · MYANMAR

Malaysia to send more than 6,500 Rohingya refugees back to Myanmar

Malaysia will return more than 6,500 Rohingya refugees to Myanmar, in a move that follows last week’s International Court of Justice ruling that Myanmar carried out state-sponsored genocide against the Rohingya minority.

The returns put fresh focus on the Rohingya crisis days after the ICJ ruling, as governments weigh how to respond to the court’s finding against Myanmar’s military.
