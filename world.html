Briefing · October 15, 2026

MIDDLE EAST · ENERGY SECURITY

Officials tell AP that Houthi strike damaged Aramco executive offices

Two regional officials told The Associated Press that an overnight strike hit offices used by senior executives at Saudi oil company Aramco in Dhahran. They said the top three floors of the building were damaged and that the company chief executive’s office was among them. The officials said there were no casualties in the strike.

The report places the strike amid a sharp escalation between the Houthi movement and the Saudi-led coalition in Yemen. The AP notes that the Houthis said they targeted Dhahran, while the Saudi-led coalition said it intercepted missiles and drones aimed at several locations. The accounts and claims are attributed to the parties and officials named in the report.

Source
Associated Press

UKRAINE · RUSSIA

Governor reports fire at Ryazan refinery following Ukrainian drone attack

Reuters reports that Ryazan’s regional governor said a large Ukrainian drone attack caused a fire at the oil refinery. The governor said the extent of the damage was being assessed. Reuters reported this was the first Ukrainian strike on a Russian energy facility since U.S. President Donald Trump announced an energy ceasefire on Sunday, but said neither side had confirmed the truce.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy told the Financial Times that Ukraine had paused energy strikes for three days to comply with U.S. wishes and would resume them because Russia was still attacking Ukrainian energy infrastructure, Reuters reported. These are attributed statements; the status of any ceasefire remains contested in the report.

Source
Reuters
Reporting on the Ryazan refinery and the reported energy-truce context · October 15, 2026.

ASIA-PACIFIC · ECONOMY

IMF projects slower Asia-Pacific growth in 2027, flags several risks

The International Monetary Fund projects Asia-Pacific growth of 4.8% in 2026 and 4.4% in 2027, compared with 5.0% in 2025. Reuters reports the IMF cited a potential correction in the AI boom, the energy-price shock linked to conflict in the Middle East, and rising global bond yields as risks to the outlook.

The IMF’s projections are regional aggregates, not a forecast for every country. Reuters notes that the fund revised its 2026 and 2027 projections upward from April while still expecting growth to moderate from 2025.

Source
Reuters
Report on the IMF’s Asia-Pacific Regional Economic Outlook · October 15, 2026.
