SHENZHEN SUMMIT · NUCLEAR RISKS

Trump, Xi and Putin issue peace declaration after first three-way summit

President Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin met in Shenzhen on Thursday and issued a four-point joint declaration, reaffirming that “a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought” and pledging dialogue on reducing nuclear risks. The declaration also covered United Nations reform, refined petroleum supplies, and limits on the International Criminal Court’s jurisdiction.

The declaration did not mention Russia’s war in Ukraine or the U.S. conflict with Iran, and Putin acknowledged it set no ambitious goals. Trump called the Shenzhen summit “bigger than Yalta,” while analysts noted the meeting produced few tangible victories beyond the symbolic statement.

UKRAINE · RUSSIA

Russian overnight barrage hits Kyiv bridges, killing eight

Russia launched one of its largest missile and drone attacks of the war overnight, firing more than 110 missiles and more than 400 drones, Ukrainian officials said. At least eight people were killed and 28 injured, and three bridges across the Dnipro in Kyiv were damaged, causing power, water and heating outages.

The attack followed Putin’s vow to retaliate for Ukrainian strikes on the bridge to Crimea, which partly destroyed its road span this week. Poland also said Russia violated its airspace overnight with three objects, allegedly two missiles and one drone, as it prepared for military exercises with allies of Ukraine.

MIDDLE EAST · RIYADH

Houthi missile hits Riyadh’s tallest tower, home of Saudi wealth fund

A Houthi missile struck Riyadh’s tallest skyscraper on Thursday, damaging several floors of the 80-storey Public Investment Fund Tower in the King Abdullah Financial District. Saudi civil defence said there were no injuries, though windows were blown out across seven to eight floors and the metal facade was bent.

The strike follows a November 8 attack that damaged an Aramco tower in the same district. The Saudi-led coalition said it had dealt with a missile threat toward Riyadh and that debris had fallen, while three sources told Reuters the tower itself was struck by an incoming Houthi missile.

JUSTICE · MYANMAR

ICJ rules Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

The International Court of Justice ruled Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, the first time the court has held a state responsible for genocide. Judges found about 10,000 killings, widespread sexual violence and the razing of villages in 2017, and ordered Myanmar to facilitate refugee returns and pay reparations.

Myanmar called the ruling unfair and not unanimous — nine of fifteen judges voted for it. Rohingya refugees in Bangladesh celebrated the ruling, though return home remains distant while civil war continues in Myanmar.

ECONOMY · GLOBAL DEBT

IMF warns global public debt is nearing 100% of world GDP

IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva warned Thursday at the APEC summit in Shenzhen that global public debt will soon reach 100% of world GDP, the highest level since World War II, and called for urgent attention to government finances. The IMF puts global public debt at about 94% of GDP now, rising toward 100% by 2029.

Georgieva said renewed inflation had prompted many countries to raise interest rates, pushing borrowing costs higher. She also said 2026 is the first year AI has had a material impact on global growth, helping offset the energy supply shock tied to Middle East turbulence.
