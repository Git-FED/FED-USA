U.S. POLITICS · UKRAINE / RUSSIA

Trump waives sweeping Russia sanctions; Zelenskiy calls the move “wrong”

President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Friday night waiving the sanctions, restrictions and duties in the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, blocking penalties that were due to take effect Sunday under a law he signed just a month earlier. The order invokes Section 115 of the law, which lets the president waive its provisions after certifying to Congress that doing so serves the national interest. The blocked measures included a 500 percent duty on Russian goods and 100 percent duties on the biggest buyers of Russian oil and gas.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said the decision “definitely helps only the Russians,” noting Russian forces struck more than 15 power generation and transmission sites on Friday. Republican Senator Thom Tillis called the waiver “beyond irresponsible,” Senate Democratic leader Chuck Schumer called it “giving the Russian economy life support,” and Moscow welcomed it — Russia’s investment envoy Kirill Dmitriev called it “a truly historic moment.”

MIDDLE EAST · GULF

Iranian drone attack kills two Italians in Kuwait; Tehran denies role

Two Italian technicians were killed on Thursday in an Iranian drone attack on a military air-defence station in Kuwait, Italy’s foreign minister said, in the second reported Iranian attack on the U.S.-allied Gulf state this month. The victims, Antonio Saccoccio, 65, and Emanuele Lo Russo, 36, worked for Rheinmetall Italia, maintaining detection antennas for the Skyguard air-defence system. Kuwait’s Defence Ministry said its forces detected and engaged several drones, and falling debris wounded a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani national.

Iran denied any involvement. Foreign Minister Abbas Araghchi called his Kuwaiti and Italian counterparts to reject the allegations and offer a joint investigation, while Iran’s military spokesman called the incident a possible false flag. The Gulf Cooperation Council condemned the attack as a dangerous escalation.

ENERGY · MIDDLE EAST

Brent nears month high as Hormuz mine attacks and Saudi refinery strike threaten supply

Brent crude futures hovered near this month’s high on Friday as ongoing attacks on tankers in the Strait of Hormuz and Saudi energy infrastructure heightened concerns over oil exports from the conflict-hit region. Brent eased 38 cents to $96.19 a barrel by 0140 GMT, after gaining nearly $2 on Thursday to settle at $96.57, its strongest close since November 6. U.S. crude stood at $85.41 a barrel; there was no WTI settlement on Thursday because of the Thanksgiving holiday.

Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two tankers exploded and caught fire after striking sea mines north of Oman’s Khasab port, one a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude. A projectile struck Saudi Aramco’s Jazan refinery on Thursday, killing a Nepalese worker and halting some operations — a day after the Houthis claimed attacks on Ras Tanura, the kingdom’s largest refinery. OPEC+ is likely to hold output targets at a meeting on Sunday.

UKRAINE · RUSSIA

Zelenskyy warns of imminent major Russian attack on Ukraine’s power grid

Russia is preparing another major attack on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure, logistics and critical services, Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Thursday, as U.S. envoy Jared Kushner said Russian President Vladimir Putin has “shown restraint” in the war by agreeing not to use nuclear weapons. Ukrainian intelligence indicates Moscow is “completing preparations for a new massive strike,” Zelenskyy said on social media, urging Ukrainians to heed air raid warnings.

Russian missile and drone attacks have battered Ukraine’s power grid since the invasion, and crews were working to restore electricity in six regions after ballistic missiles and 113 drones hit overnight. The strikes hit an apartment building and damaged a hospital, a sports facility, businesses and a gas station, officials said.

TRADE · WORLD ECONOMY

Trading partners answer new U.S. tariff proposals with anger, protest and diplomacy

Major U.S. trading partners responded with disappointment and protests while holding out the prospect of negotiations after the Trump administration rolled out a fresh round of tariffs on goods from China, the European Union and a dozen other economies. China said it reserved the right to take necessary measures; the EU rejected the claim that Europe causes overcapacity and urged Washington to honor the 15% tariff ceiling in their existing deal. Japan called the plan “regrettable,” and Singapore said it disagreed with the findings.

The proposed duties follow a Section 301 investigation into structural excess capacity. China would face up to 20%, potentially 40% if the trade truce falls apart; the EU 15%, potentially 20%; Vietnam up to 35%. Written comments are due January 5, 2027, with hearings starting January 25.
