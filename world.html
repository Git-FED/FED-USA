MIDDLE EAST · IRAN BLOCKADE

Iran vows to use all its tools against U.S. naval blockade

Iran will use all its tools to confront the U.S. naval blockade, Foreign Ministry spokesman Esmaeil Baghaei said at his weekly news conference on Monday, calling the blockade an act of military aggression. The blockade on Iran-related oil exports was imposed April 13, lifted in June after Washington reached a draft understanding with Tehran, then restored July 14 after the agreement collapsed.

Visible Iranian crude exports have fallen to near zero in recent months because of the blockade. Iran says it controls passage through the strategic Strait of Hormuz and will only reopen it when Washington meets its conditions, including halting hostilities, lifting the blockade and waiving sanctions.

UKRAINE · RUSSIA

Zelensky calls for four-way peace talks “as soon as possible”

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky said Monday that Ukraine is ready to join four-way talks with Europe, the United States and Russia as soon as possible, telling NATO lawmakers in Berlin: “We are ready to do this. Literally tomorrow.” Partners want to hold the meeting before Christmas, though its date, venue and final arrangements have not yet been confirmed.

Zelensky repeated his plea for Ukraine to join NATO, saying major questions remain about how security guarantees would work without full membership. U.S. envoy Jared Kushner has said Washington might consider offering Ukraine a security guarantee similar to NATO’s Article 5 as part of a peace settlement.

UKRAINE · PEACE TALKS

Zelensky says U.S. offered NATO membership in exchange for giving up Donbas

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelensky revealed that Washington floated NATO membership for Ukraine in return for surrendering the Donbas to Russia, after the latest round of U.S.-brokered talks with Russia in Abu Dhabi. “The Americans raised a question: ‘What if Ukraine joins NATO? Would Ukraine withdraw from Donbas?’” he told reporters. Zelensky said the linkage had never surfaced in the negotiations before.

Zelensky stressed Kyiv would not simply hand over its territory, and would put any agreement to a national referendum only if it delivered firm security guarantees, a well-funded army and money to rebuild. Russia already holds about 80% of Donetsk and nearly all of Luhansk, and insists Ukrainian forces leave the part of Donetsk they still hold.

EUROPE · SANCTIONS

EU presents 22nd sanctions package against Russia

European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen presented the EU’s 22nd package of sanctions against Russia on Monday, saying the aim is to keep pressure on Moscow to end its war of aggression in Ukraine. The package extends the transaction ban to around 60 Russian banks, cutting them off from the SWIFT system, and cracks down on crypto and payment networks used to evade sanctions.

The package also expands the ban on energy services so no European company can help Russia build, operate or repair its energy infrastructure, lists more than 70 additional shadow-fleet vessels, and proposes banning Russian teams and athletes competing under the Russian flag from events on EU territory. It still needs unanimous approval from the 27 member states.

ECONOMY · ENERGY

Oil slides nearly 4% as returning supply outweighs Gulf attacks

Crude prices deepened their slide on Monday even as Wall Street rallied, with West Texas Intermediate down about 3.5% and Brent down about 3.6%, as returning Middle East supply and the restart of Libya’s Sharara pipeline outweighed attacks on shipping and energy facilities in the Gulf. The International Energy Agency expects a global oil supply surplus of 1.7 million barrels a day in the fourth quarter, widening next year.

Weekend attacks failed to lift prices: the Houthis claimed a direct hit on Saudi Arabia’s Rabigh refinery, Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said they launched missiles and drones at U.S. military positions in Bahrain, and UK Maritime Trade Operations reported several incidents involving tankers near the Strait of Hormuz. Oil flows through the strait remained about 42% below prewar levels.
