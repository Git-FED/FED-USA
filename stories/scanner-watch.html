PUBLIC SAFETY · STANDING GUIDE

Scanner traffic is a lead, not a conclusion

This page describes a careful public-safety reporting approach. It is not a live scanner feed or an incident update.

Guide · No live feed
Status: evergreen guide
No current incident is being reported here. For an immediate threat, follow instructions from emergency services and local authorities.

Why early radio traffic can mislead

Dispatch shorthand is incomplete by design. Details may be preliminary, misheard, corrected, or absent. A unit being sent somewhere does not establish what happened, who was involved, or whether there is a continuing threat.

What belongs in a verified update

  • Information confirmed by an agency spokesperson, official alert, public record, or on-the-record source.
  • A clear time stamp and location broad enough to inform without exposing a private person.
  • Explicit attribution for claims, plus a note when key facts remain unknown.
  • Any public safety instruction stated by the responsible authority, linked to the original notice where possible.

What should stay out of a post

Do not identify private people based on radio traffic, share live tactical positions, publish unverified casualty counts, or repeat a rumor as if a dispatcher confirmed it. Do not obstruct emergency responders to obtain a quote or image.

This newsroom does not promise continuous monitoring. For emergency information, use your local emergency-management service or official public-safety channel.

Send non-urgent, evidence-backed information through the private tip channel.
