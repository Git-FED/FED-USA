SCAM SAFETY · STANDING GUIDE

A useful scam alert does more than alarm people

This page explains the reporting format. It is not a live warning about a specific scam.

Guide · Not a current alert
Status: evergreen guide
No specific scam is being reported or verified on this page. Check the date and source on any future alert before acting on it.

What a responsible alert should answer

  • What is happening? Describe the message, call, offer, or impersonation pattern in plain language.
  • Who may be targeted? Be specific only when evidence supports it; do not imply that a whole neighborhood or group is affected from one report.
  • What has been verified? Separate a reader’s report from a documented pattern, and link to a source such as a business, agency, or public record when available.
  • What can someone do now? Give concrete protective steps and the official channel for reporting or account recovery.

Safer next steps if a message feels wrong

Pause before paying, clicking, or sharing a login code. Contact the organization through a phone number or website you find independently—not the link or number in the suspicious message. If money or account access is involved, contact your bank or service provider through its official channel.

For U.S. consumer-fraud reports, the Federal Trade Commission accepts reports at ReportFraud.ftc.gov. Use emergency services for immediate danger.

What we will not publish as a “confirmed alert”

A forwarded screenshot, a single anonymous post, or a claim repeated across group chats is not enough by itself. We do not publish personal information, accuse a private person without verification and an opportunity to respond, or amplify a rumor just because it is frightening.

Have a tip? Send it privately to the newsroom, not to a public thread.
