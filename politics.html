CONGRESS

Rep. Jimmy Gomez to resign Dec. 1 after ethics panel finds sexual misconduct

Democratic Rep. Jimmy Gomez of California said Monday he will resign from Congress effective December 1, after a House Ethics Committee report found “substantial evidence” he engaged in sexual misconduct by making unwelcome sexual advances and sexual contact toward a House staffer.

The panel said Gomez’s conduct caused the staffer significant and ongoing distress and contributed to her decision to leave House employment. Gomez, who already lost his re-election bid this month to fellow Democrat Angela Gonzales-Torres, is the third member to resign this year in the face of sexual misconduct allegations. He said he disagrees with the report’s conclusions.

Why it matters

The resignation caps a year in which the House has repeatedly reckoned with sexual misconduct allegations against members of both parties, and it comes in the final days of a lame-duck Congress.

2028 PRIMARY

DNC plans 12 debates for 2028 primary, with dark-money pledge

The Democratic National Committee announced Monday it will hold 12 presidential primary debates, with the first two in June and July 2027 and the final debate in March 2028. Candidates must sign a Fair Campaign Practices Pledge denouncing super PAC and dark-money spending to take the stage, a win for progressives who have long pushed the party to crack down on big money in politics.

The party plans to bring creators and influencers into the debate process to maximize viewership, and early debates could stretch across two nights to accommodate a field capped at 20 candidates. More details, including dates, locations, media partners and qualifying criteria, will be announced in January.

SENATE

Hassan launches inquiry into Vance’s role in Venezuelan oil deal

Democratic Sen. Maggie Hassan, the incoming chair of the Senate Homeland Security and Governmental Affairs Committee, wrote Monday to Vice President JD Vance launching an inquiry into whether Vance played a role in the awarding of a Venezuelan oil contract to commodities trader Vitol.

Vance’s office has denied a reported meeting between Vance and a Vitol trader that sits at the center of the inquiry. The inquiry lands as Democrats prepare to take control of Congress in January with new oversight powers.
