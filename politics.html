HOUSE MAJORITY

Democrats secure historic House majority

Democrats have secured at least 258 seats in the U.S. House, their largest majority since 1992, after Chuck Hubbard defeated longtime Republican Rep. Virginia Foxx in North Carolina following a recount. Democrats also secured 56 Senate seats, giving the party control of both chambers when the new Congress convenes on January 3, 2027.

President Trump has said he will veto legislation passed by Democrats when they take control, but called his veto power a starting point for lawmakers who want change, pointing to healthcare as an area where both sides might meet in the middle. Democrats are preparing to take control of Congress while the lame-duck session wrestles with the December 11 funding deadline.

Why it matters

The majority matters because Democrats take control of Congress in January 2027, so Trump’s signature becomes the gate for their agenda — and the 258-seat House majority is the largest Democratic majority in more than three decades.

FUNDING DEADLINE

Shutdown deadline looms Dec. 11 as Iran war funding splits Congress

Federal funding expires December 11, and Congress has yet to agree on a replacement, with disputes over military operations against Iran and President Trump’s request for $350 billion in additional Pentagon spending the main obstacles. Senate Republicans are considering a deal with Democrats that would add military funding while limiting the president’s ability to launch new attacks against Iran.

Lawmakers have less than two weeks of legislative time left after returning from the Thanksgiving recess. Without a deal, federal funding would expire at the end of December 11, triggering a partial government shutdown. Senate Republicans have dropped a partisan bill and now seek bipartisan talks on a $1.1 trillion defense bill that includes only $69 billion of Trump’s requested increase.

CABINET

Trump keeps Lutnick after $25 million library donation, report says

President Trump was close to ousting Commerce Secretary Howard Lutnick earlier this year after Lutnick acknowledged visiting Jeffrey Epstein’s private island, but stopped talking about replacing him after Lutnick made a $25 million donation to Trump’s presidential library, the Wall Street Journal reported. Trump publicly defended Lutnick at a White House news conference on Friday, saying he believes the commerce secretary has “done some really great things.”

Lutnick’s standing took a more serious turn in recent weeks, when Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and some congressional Republicans made their dissatisfaction clear over the Commerce Department’s handling of sensitive AI technology and potential exports to China. Lutnick has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee in December.
