Briefing · November 28, 2026

ECONOMY · WHITE HOUSE

Trump demands probe of GDP data after growth is revised up to 4.3%

President Donald Trump called for an investigation into the Commerce Department after revised data showed the U.S. economy grew 4.3% in the third quarter, up from the 3.4% estimate released in October, days before the November 3 midterm elections. In a Thanksgiving Day message, Trump accused the department of underestimating growth on purpose and called the revision “a record setting error.”

Data released by the department on November 25 showed the economy grew 4.3% in the third quarter, 0.9 points higher than the 3.4% estimate released in October. Trump spent the holiday in Florida, where he played golf, met Palm Beach County officials about federal disaster funding for flooding, and called U.S. service members.

Why it matters

The revision is the strongest growth since the third quarter of 2023, and it arrived with hotter inflation — October PCE at 3.8% — complicating the Federal Reserve’s December decision and the administration’s case for new tariffs.

SENATE · DEMOCRATS

Van Hollen rules out White House run, aims to replace Schumer

Maryland Senator Chris Van Hollen said Friday he is no longer considering a run for the White House and will focus instead on his bid to become Senate Democratic leader, a day after announcing a challenge to Chuck Schumer. Van Hollen told a Denver conference that Democrats should pursue an aggressive agenda and “go toe to toe” with President Donald Trump.

The race for leader, held December 1 behind closed doors on a secret ballot, is already dividing Democrats who just made major gains in the midterm elections, winning large new majorities in the Senate and the House. Schumer and his allies argue the wins endorse the current leadership; Van Hollen and his backers in the Democratic base say they are a call for change.

Why it matters

Whoever leads Senate Democrats sets the party’s posture toward Trump in January, when Democrats take control of Congress — confrontation or negotiation — and the December 1 vote is the first test of which camp the new majority backs.

ECONOMY · FEDERAL RESERVE

December Fed hike looks near-certain as GDP hits 4.3% and inflation 3.8%

New U.S. government data released on November 25 showed the economy grew at a 4.3% annualised pace in the third quarter while annual inflation climbed to 3.8% in October, making a December interest-rate hike by the Federal Reserve look near-certain. Core PCE inflation reached 3.3%, its highest since November 2023.

Market analysis of CME interest-rate futures put the odds of a quarter-point hike on December 9 at 98.6% on Friday. Inflation-adjusted consumer spending dipped slightly in October even as incomes rose, and the U.S. Trade Representative’s proposed Section 301 tariffs on 14 economies add another inflation risk.

Why it matters

Strong growth plus re-accelerating inflation gives the Fed room to keep raising rates even as November’s weaker services data suggests the economy could enter 2027 with less momentum than the headline growth figure implies.
