U.S. POLITICS · UKRAINE / RUSSIA

Trump waives sweeping Russia sanctions; Zelenskiy calls the move “wrong” as missiles hit Ukraine’s power grid

President Donald Trump signed an executive order on Friday night waiving the sanctions, restrictions and duties in the Lindsey O. Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act of 2026, blocking penalties that were due to take effect Sunday under a law he signed just a month earlier. The order invokes Section 115 of the law, which lets the president waive its provisions after certifying to Congress that doing so serves the national interest.

Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskiy said the decision “definitely helps only the Russians,” noting Russian forces struck more than 15 power generation and transmission sites on Friday. The blocked measures included a 500 percent duty on Russian goods and 100 percent duties on the biggest buyers of Russian oil and gas.

WORLD · MIDDLE EAST

Iranian drone attack kills two Italians in Kuwait; Tehran denies role

Two Italian technicians were killed on Thursday in an Iranian drone attack on a military air-defence station in Kuwait, Italy’s foreign minister said, in the second reported Iranian attack on the U.S.-allied Gulf state this month. The victims worked for Rheinmetall Italia, maintaining detection antennas for the Skyguard air-defence system. Kuwait said its forces detected and engaged several drones, and falling debris wounded a Kuwaiti and a Pakistani national. Iran denied involvement and called for a joint investigation.

ECONOMY · ENERGY

Brent nears month high as Hormuz mine attacks and Saudi refinery strike threaten supply

Brent crude hovered near its high for the month on Friday, easing to $96.19 a barrel after settling on Thursday at $96.57, its strongest close since November 6. Iran’s Revolutionary Guards said two tankers hit sea mines near Oman’s Khasab port, one a supertanker carrying more than 2 million barrels of crude. A projectile struck Saudi Aramco’s Jazan refinery on Thursday, killing a Nepalese worker and halting some operations. OPEC+ is expected to hold output targets at a meeting on Sunday.

U.S. POLITICS · TRADE

Trading partners answer new U.S. tariff proposals with anger, protest and diplomacy

Major U.S. trading partners responded with disappointment and protests while holding out the prospect of negotiations after the Trump administration rolled out fresh Section 301 tariffs on goods from China, the European Union and a dozen other economies. China said it reserved the right to take necessary measures; the EU rejected the claim that Europe causes overcapacity and urged Washington to honor the 15% tariff ceiling in their existing deal. Japan called the plan “regrettable,” and Singapore said it disagreed with the findings.

WORLD · UKRAINE / RUSSIA

Zelenskyy warns of imminent major Russian attack on Ukraine’s power grid

Russia is preparing another major attack on Ukraine’s energy infrastructure, logistics and critical services, President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said on Thursday, as Ukrainian intelligence indicated Moscow was completing preparations for a new massive strike. Crews were still restoring power in six regions after ballistic missiles and 113 drones hit overnight. U.S. envoy Jared Kushner said Russian President Vladimir Putin has “shown restraint” by agreeing not to use nuclear weapons.

TECH · MARKETS

S&P puts Nvidia’s AA rating on watch for a possible downgrade over governance

S&P Global Ratings placed Nvidia’s AA credit rating on CreditWatch with negative implications, warning it could downgrade the company within 90 days unless concerns about corporate governance are addressed. The agency cited board independence, oversight of senior executives, succession planning and questions about potential export-control violations after CEO Jensen Huang resigned on November 12 and was reinstated on November 23. S&P expects any cut to be limited to one notch; Moody’s held its Aa1 rating steady.
