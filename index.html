U.S. POLITICS · REUTERS

Trump says veto power is a starting point for Democrats

President Donald Trump said Friday he will veto legislation passed by Democrats when they take control of Congress in 2027, but said his veto power should be seen as a starting point for lawmakers who want change. Trump said many Democrats had called him about healthcare and immigration, and pointed to healthcare as an area where both sides might meet in the middle.

Trump also said Democratic Texas Governor-elect Gina Hinojosa had called him about the border, and he thought they could find a solution.

WORLD · SHENZHEN SUMMIT

Trump, Xi and Putin issue peace declaration after first three-way summit

President Trump, Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin met in Shenzhen on Thursday and issued a four-point joint declaration, reaffirming that “a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought” and pledging dialogue on reducing nuclear risks. The declaration did not mention Russia’s war in Ukraine or the U.S. conflict with Iran, and Putin acknowledged it set no ambitious goals.

WORLD · UKRAINE / RUSSIA

Russia hits Kyiv bridges in overnight barrage, killing eight

Russia launched one of its largest missile and drone attacks of the war overnight, firing more than 110 missiles and more than 400 drones, Ukrainian officials said. At least eight people were killed and 28 injured, and three bridges across the Dnipro in Kyiv were damaged. The attack followed Putin’s vow to retaliate for Ukrainian strikes on the bridge to Crimea.

WORLD · JUSTICE

ICJ rules Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya

The International Court of Justice ruled Thursday that Myanmar committed genocide against the Rohingya, the first time the court has held a state responsible for genocide. Judges found about 10,000 killings, widespread sexual violence and the razing of villages in 2017, and ordered Myanmar to facilitate refugee returns and pay reparations. Myanmar called the ruling unfair and not unanimous.

WORLD · MIDDLE EAST

Houthi missile hits Riyadh’s tallest tower, home of Saudi wealth fund

A Houthi missile struck Riyadh’s tallest skyscraper on Thursday, damaging several floors of the 80-storey Public Investment Fund Tower in the King Abdullah Financial District. Saudi civil defence said there were no injuries. The Houthis said they targeted the financial centre twice on Thursday, following a November 8 strike that damaged an Aramco tower in the same district.

U.S. POLITICS · OVERSIGHT

Democrats put Trump administration on notice over alleged document destruction

Democrats and the White House are already in a showdown over alleged document destruction, before Democrats take control of Congress in January. Democratic lawmakers sent preservation letters across the federal government demanding officials save records and turn off auto-delete on encrypted messaging apps. The White House said the claims lack evidence and it sees no obligation to comply before January.
